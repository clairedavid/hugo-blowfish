Pulse-shape modelling for the IceCube Neutrino Observatory

== TEXT ==

During the fourth year of my Master of Engineering, I was an exchange student at Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin and got an internship at DESY-Zeuthen, Germany's national research centre for fundamental science. There I worked on the electronics of the IceCube Neutrino Observatory, whose focus is to record signals from high-energy neutrinos coming from outer space. The neutrino is an elementary particle very abundant in the universe, yet barely interacting with matter. Most of them go through Earth without being stopped! To get a chance of an interaction, the trick is to go big: the IceCube detector is an arrangement of 5,160 digital optical modules (DOMs) buried between 1,450 and 2,450 metres deep in the Antarctic ice, covering a volume of a cubic kilometre!

My role was to measure the transfer function of the DOMs' analog signal output. In short, the transfer function is the shape of the DOM's signal, its intensity over time, which the module's electronics digitize and send to the surface. Getting that shape right matters because if the intensity goes over a given threshold, a trigger flags the signal as interesting for physics, and saves it. I used the ROOT toolkit to analyze data produced by spare modules at the DESY-Zeuthen laboratory. I summarized my results in an internal report that was uploaded to the IceCube intranet.
