== TEXT ==

This course provides the basic conceptual and technical knowledge to anyone starting out in machine learning. It begins with intuition: students first guess how the main algorithms work. Then the mathematics is thoroughly derived, step by step. Finally, the students implement the algorithms from scratch, i.e. writing the equations and the algorithmic logic in Python themselves.

From linear and logistic regression to boosted decision trees and neural networks, the goal is to show there is no magic, but beautiful (and actually simple) mathematics at play, and to give students full control over how their code is written and works.

The students learn how to assess the performance of their models and optimize them. They learn how a ROC curve is built, what under- and overfitting mean, and what the bias/variance tradeoff is, again intuitively, then mathematically. Time permitting, unsupervised techniques are covered.

For courses delivered at AIMS, the students work on a STEP, i.e. a “Study Through Exploration and Presentation.” They pick a topic they want to understand in greater depth, build a small demo (with rules on AI use so as not to delegate the thinking process), and give a 10-minute presentation in the last lecture, solo or in small groups.

Assessment combines multiple-choice quizzes, audits of the tutorials (randomly selected students are tested orally on the code they submitted), and a final exam: oral for cohorts under 40, written for larger ones.
